Plans

Three plans, sized on a call.

Every plan runs on the same foundation, and every plan starts at 50 users. The plans themselves are fixed, with one price per user per month. What we size to you on a call is the support hours package, the add-ons and the projects on top.

Transparent by design

Four components on the invoice.Nothing hiding.

You always know what you're paying for, what's in the fee and what's billable before any work begins.

Platform fee

Per user, per month, one price per plan. Everything that runs on its own is in it: monitoring, patching, scaling and the published baseline. The Microsoft 365 licence is passed through at cost.

Support hours

A monthly minimum package for the work people do, with hours above it billed at your plan's rate. Every hour logged, categorized and reported monthly. Self-service password resets cost nothing.

Projects

Migrations, security builds, compliance remediation. Scoped up front, quoted separately, at a fixed price or on time and materials.

Add-ons

Optional capabilities billed per user, per device, per domain or flat monthly. Each has a licence and a one-time implementation.

Azure is billed at exactly what your Azure portal shows, in your own tenant, with no markup. You see every line.

Not in the platform fee: creating and deleting user accounts, password resets and lockouts that need the team, end-user tickets, installing applications in the master image, security configuration beyond the published baseline, new environments or host pools, user migrations, initial implementation, and manual investigation of alerts that automation cannot resolve. These use support hours or a project, and you know which before the work starts.

Hours, not adjectives.

Questions.

How does pricing work?

Four components. A per-user monthly platform fee that covers everything automated. A monthly minimum package of support hours for the human work, every hour logged. Projects, scoped and quoted separately. And optional add-ons, each with a licence and a one-time implementation. The Microsoft 365 licence and Azure are passed through at cost: Azure at exactly what your Azure portal shows, with no markup.

How do we know which plan fits?

We work it out together on the first call. We look at your size, your industry, your compliance needs and your hours, and recommend a starting point. Every plan starts at 50 users; Core runs to 300.

Can we move up later?

Yes. The foundation is the same on every plan, so moving from Core to Advanced or Enterprise doesn’t mean a redesign.

What happens outside business hours?

Core support runs Monday to Friday, 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM, your local time. On Advanced and Enterprise, critical and high-impact incidents are covered 24/7; other requests run in the same business-hours window. Automated platform monitoring, patching and alerting run 24/7/365 on every plan. Core clients who need occasional out-of-hours cover can add an emergency readiness retainer, and an incident response retainer is available on every plan. Response commitments are set in the Services Guide that forms part of your agreement.

What's an add-on, exactly?

An add-on is the licence for an extra capability. Setting it up and configuring it is scoped as a project, so you know what you’re paying for.

How do we contract?

Through three documents: the Master Services Agreement, the Quote and the Services Guide. Together they set the scope, the plan, the price per user, the support hours package and the service levels, so the terms can be read in advance. They are reviewed by counsel and change only after counsel's review.
